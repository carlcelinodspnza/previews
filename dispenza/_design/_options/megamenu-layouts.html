Services mega menu — layout options

Six ways to lay out the suite

Every option is built from the real tokens and the real chrome sheet and staged at the real dock width of 1120px — so what you see is what would ship, not a picture of it. The current panel is a two-column list of four; the complaint is that it reads as mostly empty space. Each option keeps the same eight services and the same words, and changes only the arrangement.

Option 1

Four-Column Index

The control spends 465px of height on eight items and still leaves each 520px column half empty, so the eye has to travel down four times to see the suite. This is the same eight items as a ruled 4x2 index — 257px tall at the real 1120px dock width, 221px at 1830px — one horizontal sweep, then one more. Hairline column rules (the same --ds-hairline as the head divider) replace the empty gutters with structure, and a 16px numeral gutter gives the scan an anchor per cell; descriptions stay fully visible at every width, only dropped a step to 12.5px so the titles carry the read.

Trade-offs

Honest list, all of it measured at the REAL panel width (--ds-container-max is 1120px in dispenza-tokens.css — nothing in the build overrides it to 1830, so the brief's "~1830px" only happens on an ultra-wide dock): 1. The descriptions get smaller. 13px to 12.5px, and at 1120px they wrap to two lines in a 219px column. They pass contrast and nothing is hidden, but if the owner wants those lines to sell, four columns is the wrong direction — this option treats them as confirmation, not copy. 2. One title fits by 3px. "Web design and development" measures 216px in a 219px column at 1120px with Montserrat loaded. Any copy edit, weight bump or font substitution tips it to two lines. Subgrid keeps the whole row's descriptions on one baseline when that happens, so it degrades tidily, but the row grows ~17px. 3. It is denser on purpose: 465px tall to 257px at 1120px (-45%). Less air is the point, but it is less air — the control breathes more and a reader who wants to linger will prefer it. 4. Inverted at 1830px: at that width nothing wraps (221px tall, every item one line) and each column's text fills only about half its column, so the right side of every column is empty. The rules give that space structure; they do not fill it. 5. The 01-08 numerals imply a sequence or a ranking that does not exist. They are an index device. If the owner reads "01" as the flagship service, the option invited that misread and should lose the numerals. 6. Weaker click affordance than the control. I removed the -2px lift deliberately — a bouncing cell inside a ruled grid breaks the rules' alignment — so hover is a quiet violet wash plus a hairline ring instead of a card that comes forward. 7. Growth is capped. Four columns at 1120px is the ceiling; a ninth service means three rows or smaller type. The control's 2x4 has more slack. 8. Two modern CSS features carry the polish: subgrid (cross-cell baseline alignment) and container queries (the column ladder follows the panel, not the viewport). Both verified working in the render; both have declared fallbacks (auto rows; viewport media queries under @supports not), so an old engine gets a working but slightly ragged index rather than a broken one. 9. Integration note: the root id is panel-services-four-col-index so six options can share one comparison page. On adoption it must be renamed back to panel-services to match the rail trigger's data-panel/aria-controls. The .rail-dock rules already restore the flyout positioning, hidden/is-open states and the <=1023px hide, so no other change is needed. Option 2

Semantic clusters — three labelled columns

The flat 2x4 makes the buyer read all eight titles to find the one that matches their problem; this splits them into three labelled clusters drawn from the copy itself — "Bring in demand" (Performance marketing "paid and organic demand", Advertisement design "creative sized for every placement you actually run", CRO "turning the traffic you already pay for into orders" — all three act on the traffic flow), "Build what you own" (Brand and package design "identity, packaging and in-store collateral", Web design "menu-connected sites", Integrations "menu, POS and loyalty systems" — all three are durable owned assets), and "Know what worked" (Reports "what ran, what it cost, what it returned", Consulting "operator-level advice when you run the marketing yourself" — evidence and judgement, not execution). Measured against the control at 1830px: panel height drops 465px → 373px and each item narrows 854px → 575px, so the third column also pulls the horizontal axis harder and cuts the cavernous line lengths. The existing right-hand note moves to the foot of the third cluster, where the pricing sentence semantically belongs and where it anchors the shorter column.

Trade-offs

Honest weaknesses versus the current 2x4: 1. The taxonomy is an editorial claim, and a wrong claim is worse than no claim. A flat list asserts nothing; labelled columns assert that CRO belongs to demand rather than to the site you own — defensible from its copy ("traffic you already pay for") but genuinely arguable. If the owner reads CRO as a site discipline, the menu now actively misfiles it, and Consulting sitting under "Know what worked" is the second soft spot (it is advice, not measurement). 2. The clusters are 3/3/2, so the third column is short. I anchored it with the existing pricing sentence rather than leave a hole. If the owner wants that sentence back in the head where it is today, the third column ends in visible dead space and this option looks weaker than the screenshot. 3. Moving that sentence leaves the head carrying only "OUR SUITE" — roughly 1750px of empty header width above the hairline. It is calm, but it is empty; a header-note or a right-aligned element option would fill it better. 4. Column labels are overhead for a repeat visitor. Someone who already knows the site and wants "Reports" now scans past three uppercase labels to get there. The flat 2x4 is faster for that user; clusters only pay off for a first-time buyer who knows their problem but not the service names. 5. Three columns cut item width from 854px to 575px, which reduces the airiness but does not remove it — descriptions cap at 46ch, so there is still whitespace to the right of each line. An option that uses cards, icons, or a 4-up split will fill the 1830px more aggressively than this one does. 6. It is brittle to growth. A ninth service means re-deciding the taxonomy, not just adding a row. 7. Scope note: below 1023px the locked chrome sets .rail-panel{display:none} and the mobile drawer takes over, so these cluster labels do not reach mobile unless the drawer's accordion is updated to match — a follow-on edit outside this component. The ≤1160px and ≤760px rules here only take effect on the comparison page (verified: 8/8 items visible and no horizontal overflow at 1900/1280/1160/1024/768/760/375). Option 3

List + standing panel

The control spends 1038px of content width on two ~499px columns whose text only ever fills about 340px, so roughly the right 45% of the panel is empty and the eight items have no scan structure. This reclaims that width for a persistent 300px standing panel (positioning line, Book demo, real phone digits) and spends the recovered tightness on turning the list into a numbered index with an accent rail to run the eye down. The two columns share explicit grid row tracks, so a description that wraps to two lines keeps both columns locked to one rhythm instead of drifting — measured, the whole thing lands 14px shorter than the control while doing a second job.

Trade-offs

1. THE CTA IS MOSTLY A DUPLICATE. The rail sitting ~60px above this panel already contains "Book demo" (solid) and "Call us" (ghost). The standing panel repeats Book demo verbatim. Only the phone digits are genuinely additive — the rail says "Call us", never the number. So the honest value added is one readable phone number plus one positioning line; the rest is a second instance of a button the user can already see. This is inherent to the direction, not fixable inside it. 2. IT COSTS THE LIST HORIZONTAL ROOM. The aside takes 300px + a 40px gap out of a 1038px content box, leaving ~698px for two columns (~337px each, vs ~499px in the control). Five of the eight descriptions now wrap to two lines where the control fit every one on a single line. At 1024-1120px two titles also wrap ("Conversion rate optimization", "Web design and development"). If the owner's priority is one-line-per-service crispness, the control wins on that axis. 3. THE ASIDE STILL HAS SLACK. ~14 words, a button and a phone number in a 300px x ~370px plate leaves real leftover space. I centred the group so it reads as symmetrical padding rather than a hole, but it is emptier than the list beside it. Filling it properly would need more copy, and more copy is exactly what would out-shout the links — that is the bind this direction sits in. 4. THE TWO PIECES OF COPY OVERLAP. The head note ("Pricing for all of them sits on one page") and the aside pitch ("One published price ladder...") say a similar thing 300px apart. Both are real locked copy — the pitch is verbatim from the shipping footer — so neither is invented, but a reader may notice the echo. Cutting the head note would fix it and buy back width. 5. THE NUMERALS IMPLY A RANKING THAT DOES NOT EXIST. 01-08 reads as a sequence, so "Performance marketing" looks like the flagship purely for being first. The order is inherited from the locked chrome, but the control's plain items left it ambiguous whereas numbering makes it explicit. If the eight are meant to be peers, this is a real cost of the scan rail. 6. SHARED ROW TRACKS TRADE SLACK FOR ALIGNMENT. Because the two columns share row heights, a one-line description sitting opposite a two-line one leaves trailing whitespace in its half (visible at "Brand and package design" opposite "Integrations"). I judged locked alignment worth it, but it is a deliberate purchase of small internal gaps. 7. UNVERIFIED: I could not test this inside the live rail with hover/click open-close, only as a static specimen at the real 1120px dock width with the real chrome.css loaded. The panel-open transition and the dock's mouseleave behaviour are untouched by this option but were not exercised.

Option 4

Featured + List

The control spends 1830px of panel on eight equal boxes, so nothing is promoted and the width reads as emptiness. This gives the width a job: Performance marketing becomes a stretched accent-deep slab on the left carrying an index, a flag, a 22px title, its verbatim line, two facet chips and an explicit call, while the other seven become ruled rows in a three-column list — hairline over each row, tabular index numeral, title and one-line description. The list drops from four rows to three and gains a rule-and-numeral rhythm, so the panel gets shorter and faster to scan while the lead offer finally has somewhere to sit.

Trade-offs

Honest list, worst first. (1) The promotion is hard-coded — Performance marketing is a different element with different markup from the other seven, so if the lead offer changes the owner is editing structure, not swapping a line. The 2x4 has no such coupling. (2) Seven items in three columns leaves a ragged tail: the last row holds one row and two empty cells at the widest breakpoint. It reads acceptably because the rules sit per-row rather than spanning the grid, but it is not a resolved shape. (3) The featured slab and the list will rarely be exactly the same height, so one side carries trailing space at some widths; I pinned the CTA to the card floor so the slab absorbs the difference, but this is a mitigation, not a fix. (4) Numbering 01-08 buys scannability and reuses the site's existing .svc-index idiom, but it implies a ranking the eight services do not actually have — a visitor may read 08 Reports as least important. (5) Four strings are not from the source: the "Lead service" flag, the "Paid" and "Organic" chips, and "Open the service page". The flag is an editorial claim the owner has to be willing to make in the nav; the chips are a literal decomposition of "Paid and organic" and therefore restate the sentence directly above them, which is mild redundancy. Cut all four and the card loses roughly a third of its substance. (6) For a repeat visitor who already knows which service they want, an even 2x4 is marginally faster to target than an asymmetric split, because item one lives somewhere structurally different from items two through eight. This option optimises for the first-time scanner. (7) Layout responds via container queries; on a browser without support it degrades to one stacked column — all eight links still visible and operable, but tall. (8) The accent-deep slab is the first large saturated surface in the chrome; it is token-sanctioned (the token file names #420f7b as a card ground) and adds no glow, but it does make the Services panel louder than the rest of the rail, which the other five options will not be.

Option 6

Master + Detail Rail

Measured against the rendered computed colours, not assumed. Panel ground is --ds-bg-surface-2 (#222429); the detail pane and the active row both sit on --ds-bg-warm (#1b1d22), which is what visually links a row to its pane.
