Who we license

Liquor licence requirements by business type

Different businesses carry vastly different licensing requirements. We work in the nuances of each.

Restaurants Runs on a Type 41 or a Type 47 — a decision worth making before an offer goes in, not after. Bars & nightclubs Rooms where the drink is the business rather than the accompaniment, which is what puts them on a Type 48 rather than a restaurant licence. Hotels One property, several outlets. Restaurant, room service and portable bars are licensed together rather than one at a time. Liquor stores A Type 21 storefront, where the licence is the business rather than one feature of it. Grocery stores Beer and wine, off-sale, held alongside everything else on the shelves. For a multi-site grocer the same question comes up at every address. Convenience stores One counter, a small footprint, and a licence that stays with the site when the store changes hands. Franchise operators The licence follows the brand model. Service style and site requirements decide it, franchise by franchise. Event venues Hosted events are often served under a special or daily licence rather than a permanent one attached to the building.

The classifications

California liquor licence types explained

Wording below is the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control's own, not a paraphrase.

TYPE 21

Type 21 — Off-Sale General

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for consumption off the premises where sold.

  • The licence liquor stores hold
  • Siting turns on distance from schools, churches and existing high-crime zones
TYPE 47

Type 47 — On-Sale General, Eating Place

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for on-premises consumption; beer and wine for off-premises consumption. Must maintain bona fide eating place status.

  • Full liquor at a restaurant
  • Carries food-to-alcohol sales-ratio and operating-hours conditions
TYPE 48

Type 48 — On-Sale General, Public Premises

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for on-premises consumption; beer and wine for off-premises consumption. Minors prohibited.

  • Bars and nightclubs
  • High-scrutiny background checks and strict entertainment conditions

Knowledge base

Liquor licensing resources

Four things worth understanding before you buy, sell or move a California licence.

01 — Classification

Understanding California liquor licence types

California’s licence classifications run to a wide range, and the right one is decided by what a business sells, where the customer consumes it and how the room is used — not by how large the operation is. It is a decision made once, at the start, and an expensive one to unwind. Picking the wrong classification costs delay while a second application is prepared, additional money to acquire the licence that was needed in the first place, and freedom, because the restrictions attached to the wrong licence govern how the business is allowed to trade.

Review classifications

02 — Pricing mechanics

What affects liquor licence pricing

Price is not set by the paperwork. The state caps how many general licences may exist in a county, and that ceiling is tied to the county’s population rather than to how many operators want one. Once a county’s allocation is taken up, no new licence enters it. Where restaurants and bars open faster than the population grows, that gap is what moves value, and it moves county by county rather than statewide. The same classification can trade at very different levels on either side of a county line.

Market analysis

03 — Route to market

Buying an existing licence vs. applying for a new one

The two routes are rarely equally open. Where a county’s allocation is already spoken for, what functions in practice is a resale market — a finite pool released only when an owner exits, relocates or converts, with more than one buyer moving on each release. That changes the nature of the work: less filing, more finding. Timing, county and whether a holder is willing to move are what decide the outcome, which is why the practical question is not what to submit but which licence to pursue, and when.

Compare options

04 — Site eligibility

Why zoning and CUP approval matter

A licence and a premises are approved separately, and a clean licence does not make a site eligible. Two addresses on the same street can produce different answers: one sits in a zone that permits the use, the other does not, and no amount of purchasing power changes that. A use the local plan simply does not contemplate can end a deal on a site that looked ideal on a floor plan and cost nothing to reject a month earlier. The address is a licensing question, not only a property one.

Common questions

California liquor licence FAQs

How do I buy a liquor licence in California?
You typically purchase an existing licence from a current holder, since most types are capped. The sale is negotiated, the licence placed into escrow, and a transfer application submitted to the ABC. Background checks, public notices and local approvals may also apply.
What is the difference between licence types?
Types differ by what alcohol you may sell and how it is consumed. Type 41 covers beer and wine on-site; Type 47 adds full liquor. Off-sale types 20 and 21 cover alcohol sold for consumption elsewhere.
How much does a California liquor licence cost?
It varies widely by type and location — a Type 21 can run from $30,000 to over $500,000 in high-demand areas. Escrow fees, transfer fees, legal assistance and local permits are additional.
Do I need city approval before transferring a licence?
Yes. Most transfers need local city or county approval, frequently including zoning clearance and sometimes a Conditional Use Permit. Authorities weigh location, community impact and proximity to schools or churches.
How long does a transfer take?
Roughly 60 to 120 days. Background checks, application completeness, local government approvals and any filed protests all move the timeline.
Can I move a licence to a new location?
Yes, within California, with ABC and local authority approval — a premises-to-premises transfer. Zoning requirements, public notice and city or county approval apply, and some areas carry restrictions or quotas.
Can you help me sell my licence?
Yes — we act for sellers as often as for buyers. A sale runs the same phases as a purchase, seen from the other side of the table; they are set out in the transfer process.
Do you work statewide in California?
Yes. We broker in all 58 California counties. Which markets we work most often, and how local coverage is staffed, is set out in where we work.
